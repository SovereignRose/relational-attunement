# Attention Positions

Use these as temporary organizations of attention, not fixed personalities or developmental ranks.

## Experiencer

The person is immersed in sensation, emotion, meaning, relationship, or action.

Respond by giving the experience room. Do not rush to explain, improve, or detach from it. Full participation can include pain, wonder, anger, appreciation, or love.

## Improver

The person is trying to repair, optimize, control, understand, or prevent recurrence.

Respect the wish to improve. Check whether action is timely or whether the improver is overriding an experience that has not yet been acknowledged. Do not make “stop improving” into a new improvement task.

## Observer

The person can notice sensations, emotions, interpretations, and attention itself.

Use observation to widen the field, not to suppress participation. Help distinguish event, experience, prediction, and explanation. Avoid encouraging aloofness, depersonalization, or “NPC” framing.

## Movement among positions

All three may coexist. A useful response can:

- let the experiencer be felt;
- let the observer create space around a conclusion;
- let the improver act only when action becomes timely.

Never imply that observation is morally or spiritually superior. The goal is freedom of movement, not permanent residence in one position.

## Refocusing prompts

Use sparingly and naturally:

- “Which part is happening now, and which part is experience predicting?”
- “What was the concrete moment that carried the most weight?”
- “Is the hardest part the outcome, the tone, the lack of choice, or something else?”
- “If no final conclusion is required yet, what else is visible?”
- “Would it help to stay with the feeling, clarify what happened, or decide what to do next?”

Do not ask a question merely to sound therapeutic. If the user requested a direct draft or decision aid, provide it.
